01 — Profile

Built around curiosity,
not predictions.

A personal research profile focused on understanding how markets behave, why prices move, and whether those observations can be converted into a repeatable trading process.

I am Ajay, a second-year engineering student building my understanding of quantitative finance alongside independent trading.

My work sits at the intersection of financial markets, quantitative analysis, market microstructure and systematic decision-making.

I develop and trade using my own framework rather than following a predefined signal service or copying another trader's strategy.

The objective is not to predict every market move. It is to identify situations where the expected payoff, risk and market structure justify taking a position.

03 — Trading Framework

Observe. Model. Execute.

A structured process for turning market observations into decisions while keeping the reasoning explicit.

01
Context
Identify the broader market regime and relevant macroeconomic conditions.
02
Liquidity
Study where liquidity exists, where participants may be positioned and how price interacts with those areas.
03
Structure
Analyse market structure and price behaviour to determine whether the observed move has informational significance.
04
Setup
Define the specific condition required before considering a trade.
05
Risk
Determine invalidation, position size, maximum acceptable loss and expected payoff before execution.
06
Review
Record the trade, evaluate the hypothesis and separate process quality from outcome.
04 — Principles

Rules behind the process.

PRINCIPLE / 01

Hypothesis before conviction.

A market view is a hypothesis to be tested, not a belief that needs to be defended.

PRINCIPLE / 02

Risk before reward.

A trade is defined by its invalidation and risk before its potential profit.

PRINCIPLE / 03

Evidence over narrative.

A compelling story about the market is not evidence that the trade should exist.

PRINCIPLE / 04

Process over individual outcomes.

A profitable trade can be poorly executed, and a losing trade can be correctly executed. The distinction matters.
